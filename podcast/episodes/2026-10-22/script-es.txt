El primer artículo: «Towards English - Mizo Legal Machine Translation: Corpus Creation, Analysis, and Benchmarks». Los autores son Telem Joyson Singh, Sanabam Boyshana, Sanasam Ranbir Singh, et al. Indian court proceedings are held in English, so speakers of languages with limited translation technology have limited access to them. We present the first English Mizo parallel dataset of Indian court judgments. It contains 13,168 sentence pairs from the Gauhati High Court and the Supreme Court of India. Professional native Mizo translators translated complete documents; sentence pairs were manually aligned, and two independent reviewers checked the results. Our study finds that English words are borrowed in a very selective way: official and legal terms are almost always kept in English, but words for everyday things like actions, situations, and results are usually expressed in native Mizo. Mizo diacritics are also used inconsistently across the corpus. When we fine tune the NLLB 200 1.3B model on our training data, it scores 27.47 BLEU on a balanced legal test set of 1,000 sentence pairs, an improvement of 7.16 BLEU over the base model. However, it still uses English words about three times more often than a human translator does.

El segundo artículo: «How to Use Parallel Data for Multilingual Pretraining». Los autores son Zhihong Huang, Yao Lu, Derek F. Wong, et al. High-quality human-translated parallel corpora can improve cross-lingual transfer, but are typically too small for large-scale pretraining. Machine-translated web corpora help overcome this limitation, making parallel data available at pretraining scale. Standard multilingual pretraining, however, samples source documents and their translations independently, without explicitly using their parallel structure. We investigate how to use the parallel structure of the data to improve multilingual pretraining by concatenating each document with its translation in the same training sequence. We compare this parallel pretraining (PPT) approach with standard pretraining (SPT), training models from scratch at matched token budgets. Surprisingly, despite the benefits of parallel data reported in prior cross-lingual work, PPT alone does not consistently improve language understanding over SPT.

El tercer artículo: «UvA-MT's Participation in the WMT26 General Translation Shared Task». Los autores son Di Wu, Sergey Troshin, Wafaa Mohammed, et al. This paper presents UvA-MT's submission to the WMT 2026 General Machine Translation shared task, competing in the unconstrained track across all 23 translation directions. This year, we fully leverage the test-time methods of Large Language Models (LLMs) for machine translation. Specifically, (1) we use sequential sampling with a refinement prompt to generate a pool of translation candidates for each source segment, a strategy shown to outperform traditional parallel sampling; and (2) we employ LLMs as pairwise judges to select the best candidate via a round-robin voting mechanism, with offline evaluation on the WMT25 benchmark showing a very clear improvement over point-wise best-of-N selection. Combined, these two strategies form our WMT26 submission.

El cuarto artículo: «Hybrid Momentum Methods for Optimizing Deep Learning Models». Los autores son Haifeng Xu, Yijin Ren, Chenyu Zhou, et al. Momentum is central to stochastic optimization in deep learning, including the training of large language models. Classical momentum methods typically rely on a fast exponential moving average (EMA) scheme, which discounts past gradients on a fixed timescale and may underweight older gradients that remain informative. This creates a trade-off between retaining long-term history and adapting to recent changes. We introduce Hybrid Momentum (HyM), which combines a linearly weighted average of past gradients with a geometrically discounted fast momentum. The former assigns weights proportional to the gradients' iteration indices and retains a growing span of history, while the latter emphasizes recent gradients. This hybrid construction is modular and can be combined with adaptive preconditioning and other update transformations. In particular, we propose HyMUON, which applies Newton--Schulz orthogonalization to the hybrid direction. Illustrative examples and controlled experiments demonstrate the benefits of combining gradient information across these two timescales.
