Der erste Beitrag: „MIDI-REPA: Improving Text-to-Music Generation with Symbolic Representation Alignment“. Die Autorinnen und Autoren sind Song-Ze Yu, Fang-Duo Tsai, Yi-Hsuan Yang. State-of-the-art text-to-music models increasingly rely on scaling model capacity, data, and compute, while still learning musical structure directly from dense audio. Yet melody, rhythm, harmony, and structure can be represented compactly in symbolic form. We introduce MIDI-REPA, a representation-alignment framework that derives MIDI supervision from automatic music transcription (AMT) to align intermediate representations of a flow-matching DiT. Building on the 122M-parameter FluxAudio-S baseline from the 2026 ICME Academic Text-To-Music Challenge, we investigate what, where, and when to align. Aligning a MuScriptor-derived piano roll with an early DiT layer throughout the full flow trajectory performs the best. Without additional training data or techniques, MIDI-REPA remains competitive on the ATTM objective metrics while remaining trainable on a single RTX 3090 in about 31 hours. Our results highlight symbolic supervision as a simple and inexpensive learning signal for improving text-to-music generation.

Der zweite Beitrag: „MUSEBENCH: A Benchmark for AI-Generated Music Detection and Artifact Analysis“. Die Autorinnen und Autoren sind Yi Chen Liu, Syed Irfan Ali Meerza, Jiawei Yu et al. Advances in generative AI have made it possible to produce music that is nearly indistinguishable from human compositions, raising the question of whether such works can be reliably distinguished from human compositions. We present MUSEBENCH, a large-scale benchmark for AI-generated music detection and artifact analysis, comprising over 361K tracks. The dataset includes human compositions, stylistically matched music generated by ten state-of-the-art open-source and commercial models, such as Suno and Udio, as well as human-AI co-creations and variants processed with adversarial perturbations, compression, and acoustic post-processing. Using MUSEBENCH, we benchmark 11 automated detectors and evaluate their accuracy, generalization, and robustness, under default, out-of-domain, and transformed-variant experiment settings. We further conduct a comprehensive artifact analysis through acoustic feature inspection, identifying recurring signatures such as non-idiomatic harmonic progressions, upsampling induced spectral periodicities, and violations of physical performance constraints.

Der dritte Beitrag: „MSVAE: Rethinking Musical Semantics in Continuous Latent Spaces“. Die Autorinnen und Autoren sind Guobin Ma, Yuepeng Jiang, Dake Guo et al. Continuous latent representations underlie recent diffusion-based lyrics-to-song and text-to-music systems, yet aligning lyric conditions with these latents remains difficult: existing systems resort to generator-side machinery such as sentence-level timestamps, auxiliary alignment objectives, or semi-autoregressive decoding, while the latents they predict are still produced by autoencoders trained purely for reconstruction. We address this difficulty from the representation side. We propose MSVAE (Music Semantic Variational Autoencoder), which structures its continuous latent around two complementary semantic streams: work-level compositional content and lyric-level linguistic-vocal content. Partitioned dual-stream alignment makes both streams explicit by aligning disjoint latent subspaces with frozen lead-sheet and ASR references, while a semantic-first gated residual posterior derives its base from a frozen music-SSL encoder and admits acoustic information only through gated, bounded corrections.

Der vierte Beitrag: „TASTE: Taxonomy-Aware Symbolic Piano Generation from Textual Description“. Die Autorinnen und Autoren sind Mingyang Yao, Jingyue Huang, Ke Chen et al. Text-conditioned symbolic music generation has attracted growing interest with the development of generative models. Yet two questions remain underexplored: what aspects of music the conditioning text should describe, and how the corresponding data should be collected and trained to improve controllability and text-music alignment. We address these challenges with TASTE, a taxonomy-aware framework for symbolic music generation with a main focus on piano pieces. First, we introduce a taxonomy of musical elements that specifies how a piece can be captured through textual description in a musically grounded way. The taxonomy comprises a set of metadata tags (genre, emotion, section plan, etc.) together with a free-form description of music imagery. This structure decomposes textual input to match the generation goal more coherently and precisely. Second, we build a data pipeline for collecting, annotating, and retrieving public symbolic piano resources, yielding a corpus of 432K MIDI files labeled at multiple levels of granularity.
