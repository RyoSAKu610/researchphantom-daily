Il primo articolo: «Reliability-Aware Probabilistic Fusion for Multimodal Learning with Incomplete Evidence». Gli autori sono Patitapaban Palo, Abhisek Tiwari, Mattia Carletti, et al. Multimodal inputs may be missing or degraded, raising distinct questions of evidence availability and trust. We introduce a reliability-aware Gaussian Product-of-Experts (PoE) in which an input-dependent reliability weight tempers each modality's latent precision, while missing experts are omitted. We characterize how reliability and availability affect posterior information and expert influence. Across two clinical multimodal tasks, the full framework remains competitive with complete inputs and achieves its clearest mean-AUROC advantage under modality absence, particularly on HAIM-MIMIC-MM.

Il secondo articolo: «Reusing Multimodal Agents: Efficient Evaluation of Sound Designs in Games». Gli autori sono Thai Nguyen Van, Ibrahim Khan, Mury Fajar Dewantoro, et al. This paper introduces a novel framework for efficiently evaluating in-game sound designs using multimodal learning and reinforcement learning. Sound design significantly influences gameplay and accessibility; however, objective evaluation methods, defined here as automated assessments based on agent performance rather than human ratings, often require retraining agents from scratch for each sound design. Our approach first pre-trains a multimodal encoder that encodes visual and auditory information into a shared latent space. A reinforcement learning agent is then trained using this latent representation.

Il terzo articolo: «MOFFieldBench: A Machine Learning Benchmark for Continuous Electronic Fields in Metal-Organic Frameworks». Gli autori sono Zhaolin Hu, Hehe Fan, Chenhao Rao, et al. Machine-learning models for metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) predominantly rely on atomic structures, whereas electron-density (ED) and electrostatic-potential (ESP) fields remain underexplored as scalable learning representations. These continuous fields encode complementary spatial and electronic information relevant to structure-property relationships, particularly pore geometry and guest-framework interactions, but costly first-principles calculations, storage requirements and the scarcity of systematically aligned datasets limit their broader use.

Il quarto articolo: «CheXFuse: Guided Differential Joint Attention Fusion for Robust Multimodal Chest X-Ray Classification in Data-Scarce Medical Settings». Gli autori sono Muntaqim Ahmed Raju, Priyanka Siddappa, Md Shifat Haider Al Amin, et al. A central challenge in multimodal learning is how to fuse modalities that differ substantially in predictive strength. When one modality provides a much stronger representation than another, indiscriminate cross-modal interaction can introduce weak, redundant, or unstable signals, limiting or even degrading performance relative to the strongest unimodal representation. To address this problem, we propose CheXFuse, a multimodal fusion framework based on Guided Differential Joint Attention (GuDJA).

Il quinto articolo: «MM-LeJEPA: Correlated Gaussian Regularization for Multimodal Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures». Gli autori sono Haowen Bai, Rui Gong$, Meichen Liu, et al. LeJEPA derives an isotropic Gaussian embedding target by minimizing, under its task assumptions, a Fisher information criterion that controls the task-averaged squared prediction bias. This enables unimodal self-supervised learning without reconstruction decoders, exponential-moving-average teachers, or contrastive negatives. However, its isotropic task prior does not account for the cross-modal structure of multimodal tasks that read shared content. We introduce MM-LeJEPA by extending LeJEPA's task assumptions to multimodal learning.

Il sesto articolo: «AdamPM: Memory-Efficient LLM Optimization via Moment Pooling». Gli autori sono Xiaowei Ji, Lishan Xu, Jianing An, et al. Adam is widely used for training large language models (LLMs) owing to its strong optimization performance and training stability, but its element-wise estimation for the first and second moments incur substantial memory overhead. Although numerous memory-efficient optimizers have been proposed, many incur additional computational overhead or require additional tuning of hyperparameters. Consequently, Adam remains a dominant and reliable choice for LLM pre-training.
