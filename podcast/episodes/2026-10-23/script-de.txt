Der erste Beitrag: „Reliability-Aware Probabilistic Fusion for Multimodal Learning with Incomplete Evidence“. Die Autorinnen und Autoren sind Patitapaban Palo, Abhisek Tiwari, Mattia Carletti et al. Multimodal inputs may be missing or degraded, raising distinct questions of evidence availability and trust. We introduce a reliability-aware Gaussian Product-of-Experts (PoE) in which an input-dependent reliability weight tempers each modality's latent precision, while missing experts are omitted. We characterize how reliability and availability affect posterior information and expert influence. Across two clinical multimodal tasks, the full framework remains competitive with complete inputs and achieves its clearest mean-AUROC advantage under modality absence, particularly on HAIM-MIMIC-MM. Ablations show a substantial gain from missing-aware training and an additional HAIM improvement from reliability weighting under matched PoE, missing-aware, and consistency training. Unseen test-time corruptions are used diagnostically and reveal modality-dependent reliability-weight and latent-uncertainty responses, including failure under severe degradation.

Der zweite Beitrag: „Reusing Multimodal Agents: Efficient Evaluation of Sound Designs in Games“. Die Autorinnen und Autoren sind Thai Nguyen Van, Ibrahim Khan, Mury Fajar Dewantoro et al. This paper introduces a novel framework for efficiently evaluating in-game sound designs using multimodal learning and reinforcement learning. Sound design significantly influences gameplay and accessibility; however, objective evaluation methods, defined here as automated assessments based on agent performance rather than human ratings, often require retraining agents from scratch for each sound design. Our approach first pre-trains a multimodal encoder that encodes visual and auditory information into a shared latent space. A reinforcement learning agent is then trained using this latent representation. We fine-tune the multimodal encoder for each sound design while keeping the trained policy fixed, allowing us to isolate the effects of sound changes on agent behavior. To evaluate the proposed method, we conducted experiments on games from multiple genres, comparing the performance of blind agents (agents using only sound input) under various sound designs.

Der dritte Beitrag: „MOFFieldBench: A Machine Learning Benchmark for Continuous Electronic Fields in Metal-Organic Frameworks“. Die Autorinnen und Autoren sind Zhaolin Hu, Hehe Fan, Chenhao Rao et al. Machine-learning models for metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) predominantly rely on atomic structures, whereas electron-density (ED) and electrostatic-potential (ESP) fields remain underexplored as scalable learning representations. These continuous fields encode complementary spatial and electronic information relevant to structure-property relationships, particularly pore geometry and guest-framework interactions, but costly first-principles calculations, storage requirements and the scarcity of systematically aligned datasets limit their broader use. Here we introduce MOFFieldBench, a multimodal benchmark comprising approximately 46,000 MOFs with one-to-one aligned crystallographic structures, ED fields and ESP fields. A unified workflow integrates field generation, error-controlled compression and sparse point-cloud representation, supporting field reconstruction, prediction of 13 material properties, multimodal property prediction and pairwise cross-modal retrieval. ED and ESP provide competitive representations for pore geometry, adsorption and separation properties.
Ein Abstract verrät nicht, wie stark die Ergebnisse sind; sehen Sie sich direkt die Bewertungsgrundlagen und den Gültigkeitsbereich der Experimente an.
Der vierte Beitrag: „CheXFuse: Guided Differential Joint Attention Fusion for Robust Multimodal Chest X-Ray Classification in Data-Scarce Medical Settings“. Die Autorinnen und Autoren sind Muntaqim Ahmed Raju, Priyanka Siddappa, Md Shifat Haider Al Amin et al. A central challenge in multimodal learning is how to fuse modalities that differ substantially in predictive strength. When one modality provides a much stronger representation than another, indiscriminate cross-modal interaction can introduce weak, redundant, or unstable signals, limiting or even degrading performance relative to the strongest unimodal representation. To address this problem, we propose CheXFuse, a multimodal fusion framework based on Guided Differential Joint Attention (GuDJA). CheXFuse combines image and text token representations within a joint multimodal sequence, where learned token guidance emphasizes informative representations and differential attention reduces less useful attention responses. We evaluate CheXFuse on the Indiana University (IU) Chest X-ray dataset, using binary (normal vs. abnormal) classification as the downstream task. Experiments are conducted under both original-report and masked-report settings, with the latter serving as a controlled stress test under reduced textual informativeness.
