Der erste Beitrag: „Raise or Check? Predicting the Necessity of Additional Fuzzing for Continuous Fuzzing“. Die Autorinnen und Autoren sind Riku Kato, Tatsuya Shirai, Olivier Nourry et al. Continuous fuzzing is used for vulnerability detection in Open Source Software (OSS). Large-scale services such as Google's OSS-Fuzz fuzz over 1,300 projects and have revealed more than 10,000 vulnerabilities. Despite this, a considerable time lag often exists between when a vulnerability is introduced and when it is detected (i.e., the window of exposure). Given the computational cost of additional fuzzing, we aim to allocate fuzzing resources efficiently using vulnerability-risk estimates. We propose RAISE (Risk-Aware Intensive Supplemental Execution Framework), a Just-In-Time (JIT) framework that prioritizes additional fuzzing for high-risk project-days. RAISE performs binary classification at the project-day granularity to estimate whether a vulnerability is introduced on a given day.

Der zweite Beitrag: „Towards Robust Model Organisms for Eliciting Latent Knowledge“. Die Autorinnen und Autoren sind Lennart Finke, Hardik Bhatnagar, Euodia Dodd et al. A key problem in AI safety is eliciting latent knowledge (ELK), extracting information from a model that is misaligned and more knowledgeable than its overseer. For example, a misaligned model used to scan code for vulnerabilities may want to withhold its findings, i.e., sandbag, in order to exploit them later. To study ELK empirically, we need model organisms that mirror this setting. Existing model organisms fall short in several respects. In this work, we train model organisms to withhold solutions in a vulnerability detection task.

Der dritte Beitrag: „How Effectively Do Security Skills Guide Coding Agents in Vulnerability Detection in Linux Kernel?“. Die Autorinnen und Autoren sind Haoran Xue, Nikta Akbarpour, Gias Uddin et al. Large language model (LLM) coding agents, such as Claude Code, are increasingly used for security auditing, with task-specific skills providing reusable vulnerability-analysis procedures. However, their effectiveness in detecting real vulnerabilities and the reasons for their failures remain unclear. We conduct the first large-scale empirical study of 37 security skills using Claude Code with four underlying LLMs on 100 real Linux kernel vulnerabilities across ten classes, totaling 14800 runs. We evaluate vulnerability detection at the function, statement, and vulnerability-class levels. Results show that 24.4 percent of runs locate the correct function, 18.3 percent identify both the function and responsible statement, and 15.7 percent correctly classify the vulnerability.

Der vierte Beitrag: „The One-Pixel of Reasoning: One Truthful Relation Is Enough to Mislead LLM Agents“. Die Autorinnen und Autoren sind Jinwei Hu, Youcheng Sun, Yi Dong et al. Can one sentence manipulate an agent's belief by targeting a single implicit warrant linking evidence to a conclusion? In this paper, we introduce the one-relation attack (ORA), a novel cognitive attack on this inferential relation. ORA localizes a critical implicit warrant and performs a one-relation intervention using a single source-grounded sentence that qualifies this warrant and steers the agent toward an incorrect belief. Across eighteen models on biomedical inference and vulnerability detection, best-of-$10 attacks overturn initially correct answers at mean rates of 54.9% for proprietary and 80.0% for open-weight models. Attacks also transfer without victim feedback, and paired controls support the roles of targeting and qualification.

Der fünfte Beitrag: „Beyond Confidence: Rethinking Uncertainty Calibration in Deep Code Models“. Die Autorinnen und Autoren sind Ravishka Rathnasuriya, Wei Yang. Deep learning models have shown significant promise in code-related tasks. While confidence calibration techniques have been proposed to improve model reliability, their effectiveness in real-world tasks with strict accuracy requirements remains uncertain. In this paper, we investigate two key questions: first, whether existing measurement metrics, including confidence scores and uncertainty metrics, are effective for guiding selective prediction; and second, whether existing calibration techniques can improve these metrics to better identify cases where model predictions are likely to succeed or fail. We conduct a systematic study on defect prediction and vulnerability detection using seven code models, evaluating thirteen uncertainty signals and three widely used post-hoc calibration methods.
