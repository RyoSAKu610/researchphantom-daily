State-of-the-art text-to-music models increasingly rely on scaling model capacity, data, and compute, while still learning musical structure directly from dense audio. Yet melody, rhythm, harmony, and structure can be represented compactly in symbolic form. We introduce MIDI-REPA, a representation-alignment framework that derives MIDI supervision from automatic music transcription (AMT) to align intermediate representations of a flow-matching DiT. Building on the 122M-parameter FluxAudio-S baseline from the 2026 ICME Academic Text-To-Music Challenge, we investigate what, where, and when to align.

Advances in generative AI have made it possible to produce music that is nearly indistinguishable from human compositions, raising the question of whether such works can be reliably distinguished from human compositions. We present MUSEBENCH, a large-scale benchmark for AI-generated music detection and artifact analysis, comprising over 361K tracks. The dataset includes human compositions, stylistically matched music generated by ten state-of-the-art open-source and commercial models, such as Suno and Udio, as well as human-AI co-creations and variants processed with adversarial perturbations, compression, and acoustic post-processing.

Continuous latent representations underlie recent diffusion-based lyrics-to-song and text-to-music systems, yet aligning lyric conditions with these latents remains difficult: existing systems resort to generator-side machinery such as sentence-level timestamps, auxiliary alignment objectives, or semi-autoregressive decoding, while the latents they predict are still produced by autoencoders trained purely for reconstruction. We address this difficulty from the representation side.

Yet two questions remain underexplored: what aspects of music the conditioning text should describe, and how the corresponding data should be collected and trained to improve controllability and text-music alignment.
