El primer artículo: «Formally Verified Autoresearch for Analyzing the Expressivity of Multi-Head Attention». Los autores son Karthik Viswanathan, Plamen Dimitrov, Stefano Rocca, et al. Multi-head attention is a central component of the transformer architecture, yet how its expressivity depends on the number of heads remains poorly understood. We study a one-layer, attention-only transformer with additive positional embeddings and define the head complexity of a Boolean function as the minimum number of attention heads required to represent it.

El segundo artículo: «Repair, Retry, and the Cost of Hindsight: Verifier Feedback in Formal Theorem Proving». Los autores son Jack Li, Sophia Li. Verifier-guided self-repair conditions a model on feedback from failed attempts before it tries again, without weight updates. Yet two findings in prior work are in tension: verifier feedback can improve repair of failures, but self-repair itself can provide little-to-no advantage over independent resampling.

El cuarto artículo: «Lean Graph Search for Formal Theorem Proving». Los autores son Yihan Xu, Jia-Chen Gu, Junyi Zhang, et al. Retrieving relevant lemmas is crucial for formal theorem proving, enabling provers to build machine-checkable proofs from established mathematical results. Recent retrieval systems match library declarations to a target theorem or its intermediate proof steps, but make limited use of multi-hop dependencies among declarations.

El quinto artículo: «Learning Lean Feedback for Efficient Proof-Candidate Evaluation on GPUs». Los autores son Lazar Milikic, Etienne Bamas, Emmanuel Abbe, et al. Training language models for formal theorem proving requires large numbers of verifier calls. Slow checks and timeouts often leave GPUs waiting and withhold rewards from potentially correct proofs. We train neural evaluators to predict Lean 4 verdicts and compiler feedback on GPUs using 4.84M labelled programs. We compare two validity classifiers with a feedback model that annotates proof attempts with predicted goal states and error messages.
