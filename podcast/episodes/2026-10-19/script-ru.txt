Первая статья: «Formally Verified Autoresearch for Analyzing the Expressivity of Multi-Head Attention». Авторы: Karthik Viswanathan, Plamen Dimitrov, Stefano Rocca и др. Multi-head attention is a central component of the transformer architecture, yet how its expressivity depends on the number of heads remains poorly understood. We study a one-layer, attention-only transformer with additive positional embeddings and define the head complexity of a Boolean function as the minimum number of attention heads required to represent it. We derive an exact linear-fractional normal form for head complexity, prove a general threshold-degree lower bound, and give an explicit ten-bit function for which this bound is strict.

Вторая статья: «Repair, Retry, and the Cost of Hindsight: Verifier Feedback in Formal Theorem Proving». Авторы: Jack Li, Sophia Li. Verifier-guided self-repair conditions a model on feedback from failed attempts before it tries again, without weight updates. Yet two findings in prior work are in tension: verifier feedback can improve repair of failures, but self-repair itself can provide little-to-no advantage over independent resampling. We reconcile these findings in Lean theorem proving through controlled one-step experiments that hold the preceding failure fixed while varying whether the failed proof is shown, whether the compiler error corresponds to that failure, and the retry instruction.

Третья статья: «At the Limits of Computability: Steps Towards Autonomous AI Discovery on the Busy Beaver Frontier». Авторы: Marco Dos Santos, Vivek Veeriah, Alex Havrilla и др. Recent breakthroughs in AI for mathematics---ranging from Olympiad medal performances to resolving long-standing open conjectures---have rapidly expanded what automated systems can achieve. Yet these successes primarily concern predefined, human-formulated problems, leaving open how AI systems can autonomously uncover mathematical structure and formulate new questions. To push beyond static challenges, we explore the Busy Beaver (BB) domain, an uncomputable frontier that is fundamentally open-ended.

Четвёртая статья: «Lean Graph Search for Formal Theorem Proving». Авторы: Yihan Xu, Jia-Chen Gu, Junyi Zhang и др. Retrieving relevant lemmas is crucial for formal theorem proving, enabling provers to build machine-checkable proofs from established mathematical results. Recent retrieval systems match library declarations to a target theorem or its intermediate proof steps, but make limited use of multi-hop dependencies among declarations. These dependencies can reveal supporting lemmas that are not necessarily lexically similar to the target theorem but recur in proofs of related problems.

Пятая статья: «Learning Lean Feedback for Efficient Proof-Candidate Evaluation on GPUs». Авторы: Lazar Milikic, Etienne Bamas, Emmanuel Abbe и др. Training language models for formal theorem proving requires large numbers of verifier calls. Slow checks and timeouts often leave GPUs waiting and withhold rewards from potentially correct proofs. We train neural evaluators to predict Lean 4 verdicts and compiler feedback on GPUs using 4.84M labelled programs. We compare two validity classifiers with a feedback model that annotates proof attempts with predicted goal states and error messages.

Шестая статья: «Do Theorem Provers Need Theorem-Proving Agents? mini-Lean-agent: A Coding-Agent Baseline for Lean». Авторы: Tomas Ortega, Simon Park, Ayush Khaitan и др. General-purpose coding agents use standard shell and file interactions, yet formal theorem-proving agents often rely on specialized, ad-hoc tooling. This makes it difficult to determine which components of theorem-proving agents provide benchmark gains: an improved base model, a specialized harness, a specific tool, or a combination of any of them. We introduce mini-Lean-agent, a deliberately minimal agent for Lean tasks whose only model-visible tool is bash.
