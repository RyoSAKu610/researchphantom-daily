El primer artículo: «Physical Canvas: A Vision Tactile World Model For Robot Policy Evaluation And Learning». Los autores son Zebin Xing, Yuhang Zheng, Yupeng Zheng, et al. Evaluating and post-training robot policies through physical interaction is costly and difficult to scale, motivating action-conditioned world models as interactive simulators. However, visually plausible predictions can misrepresent contact dynamics, providing unreliable feedback for policy evaluation and learning. We introduce PhysCanvas, a state-consistent visuo-tactile world model for contact-rich robot manipulation.

Human-collected real-world robotic manipulation demonstrations often contain mixed-quality trajectories, including hesitant, indirect, or inefficient action segments. While diffusion- and flow-based policies can model such multimodal data, standard behavior cloning (BC) assigns equal likelihood to all demonstrated actions and may therefore reproduce suboptimal modes.

El séptimo artículo: «HATCH: Hardware-Attested Task Chains for Verifiable Manipulation». Los autores son Shiza Charania. Capability claims in robot manipulation are unverifiable, and existing leaderboards are each closed, simulated, or gameable. We propose HATCH, a robot escape room: an openly specified, physically replicable, sensor-scored, and unrehearsable benchmark. Tasks span five skill categories at three difficulty levels and are chained so that the sensor confirming one task's completion unlocks the next, turning a room into a single long-horizon evaluation.
