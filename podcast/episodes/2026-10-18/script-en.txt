First paper: "LIBERO-PeRM: Benchmarking Personalized Robotic Manipulation". The authors are Zhixu Li, Keqian Tang, Litian Gong, et al.. Personalization is essential for general-purpose robots to transition into household environments and achieve true practicality. While general robotic manipulation has been extensively studied, personalized robotic manipulation (PeRM) lacks a controlled benchmark for separating task completion from preference satisfaction. We introduce LIBERO-PeRM, a benchmark for preference learning in embodied agents. LIBERO-PeRM studies five questions: 1) how policies follow preferences in zero-shot settings; 2) how they learn single preferences from demonstrations; 3) how efficiently they acquire preferences as demonstrations increase; 4) how learned preferences generalize across layouts and tasks; and 5) how policies compose preferences and behave under conflicts.

Second paper: "Sim-to-Real Reinforcement Learning for Path-Safe Target Reaching in Robotic Harvesting of Hydroponic Lettuce". The authors are Al Bashir, Partho Ghose, Yibin Wang, et al.. Robotic harvesting requires precise manipulation to ensure accurate positioning of the end-effector, especially in a constraint workspace. This study introduces the Path-Safe Approach Proximal Policy Optimization (PSA-PPO), a target-conditioned local reaching framework designed for robotic harvesting of nutrient film technique (NFT)-hydroponic lettuce in greenhouses. The proposed PSA-PPO preserves the standard clipped PPO objective while introducing a harvesting-oriented policy formulation that integrates target-conditioned observations, corridor-aware spatial constraints, trajectory-history-based safety encoding, and task-specific success criteria for constrained robotic manipulation.

Third paper: "Mem4Act: Learning Effective Latent Memory for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation". The authors are Yun Zhang, Zhiyu Huang, Tianhui Cai, et al.. Long-horizon robotic manipulation fundamentally requires memory: agents must retain task-relevant information from past observations and interactions and use it to guide future actions. Existing approaches typically preserve raw historical observations, compressed visual tokens, or language summaries, but these representations either expose the action policy to increasingly long histories or risk discarding task-relevant temporal, semantic, and perceptual information. We introduce Mem4Act, a framework for learning effective latent memory as a fixed-size representation explicitly optimized for long-horizon robotic control. Mem4Act addresses three coupled challenges: how to efficiently cover long histories, what information memory should retain, and how to ensure the policy actually relies on memory.

Fourth paper: "MOSS: Mode-Oriented Source Shaping for Robot Flow Matching Policies". The authors are Zirun Ma, Jindou Jia, Tuo An, et al.. In robotic manipulation, the same observation often admits multiple feasible actions. Generative policies such as diffusion and flow matching naturally capture such multimodal behaviors, yet which mode is executed is left to random sampling and cannot be actively controlled. We propose Mode-Oriented Source Shaping (MOSS), which turns mode selection into an explicit choice of where generation starts. We first reveal, both empirically and theoretically, that deterministic flow transport induces mode-dependent structure in the source space. Under ideal deterministic ODE transport, distinct behavioral modes have distinct source-space preimages, while practical approximation errors can blur their boundaries due to imbalanced data, limited model capacity, and few inference steps.

Fifth paper: "FastVLA: Task-Conditioned and Temporally Adaptive Visual Token Compression for Efficient Vision-Language-Action Models". The authors are Jiuyi Xu, Chengyuan Qian, Zhengzhong Tu, et al.. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models present a promising paradigm for general-purpose robot manipulation, but closed-loop deployment is often limited by inference efficiency: at each timestep, high-resolution, often multi-view images are encoded into hundreds of visual tokens that dominate transformer computation and latency. Token compression via attention is a natural solution but is challenging for VLA control because (1) decision-critical tokens are determined by the task, not generic visual saliency, and (2) the token budget varies substantially across steps, so static metrics and budgets waste computation or discard control-critical tokens. We introduce FastVLA, a training-free, task-aware, and temporally adaptive visual token compression framework.

Sixth paper: "Sub-Millisecond Reflexes for Continuous Physical Human-Robot Interaction". The authors are Jon Škerlj, Sami Haddadin. Continuous physical human-robot interaction requires robots to rapidly adapt their control behaviour without interrupting task execution. However, existing ROS 2 control architectures rely on stop-to-switch mechanisms that introduce substantial delays and interrupt execution. We present an open-source, sub-millisecond reflex engine based on an enhanced ROS 2-compatible multimode controller that enables deterministic switching between control modalities and integrates reflex behaviours for mitigating contact hazards and kinematic limit violations. Experiments on a Franka Robotics manipulator demonstrate switching delays below 0.1 ms, more than four orders of magnitude faster than conventional ros2_control, while maintaining continuous operation during safety-critical events.
