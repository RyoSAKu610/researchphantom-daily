Il primo articolo: «Causal inference in staggered adoption panels: The correct comparison set depends on your intervention». Gli autori sono Ian Lundberg, Yaling Xu. A powerful data structure for causal inference is staggered adoption: many units are observed over many time periods, during which some units adopt an irreversible treatment at various time points while others remain untreated. Popular methods that apply in this setting include difference in difference, fixed effects, matching, and synthetic control. All of these methods compare the future outcomes of treatment adopters and non-adopters to answer the question: for units who adopt treatment, what outcomes would have been realized if they had remained untreated?

Il secondo articolo: «PriMa-Causa: Causal Prescriptive Maintenance». Gli autori sono Felix Saretzky. Automated manufacturing requires data-driven decisions under uncertainty and sparse observational records, and unplanned downtime is a dominant cost of getting them wrong. Prescriptive maintenance recommends a maintenance action against an impending failure rather than merely predicting it. Established reference architectures couple predictive models with rule- or optimisation-based recommendation logic. Selecting a corrective action, however, requires an interventional quantity: how the line behaviour would change if the action were set.

Il terzo articolo: «Estimand frameworks for causal inference in clinical research and regulatory decision making». Gli autori sono Miguel A. Hernán, Mats J. Stensrud, Xabier García-Albéniz, et al. Causal inference for clinical and regulatory decision making requires a precise definition of the causal estimand, that is, what we want to estimate. Three frameworks are commonly used to describe causal estimands: PICO and its extensions, the target trial framework, and the E9(R1) Addendum of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH). Because these frameworks were developed by different scientific communities, they operate at different levels of detail, emphasize different aspects of the causal estimand, and use different terminology for closely related concepts.
Definizioni di termini e limiti del metodo si verificano meglio con le parole degli autori. Se vi interessa, aprite il preprint prima di proseguire.
Il quarto articolo: «A Semi-Analytical Inference Framework for Structural Causal Models». Gli autori sono Tatiana Boura, Robert Peharz. Structural Causal Models (SCMs) provide a principled framework for observational, interventional, and counterfactual reasoning, all of which rely on probabilistic inference. Existing frameworks either transform SCMs into alternative tractable representations or rely on approximate inference with expressive generative models. We instead perform inference using a message passing scheme directly on the SCM by representing structural mechanisms and probability densities in a common finite-dimensional function space.

Il quinto articolo: «A plug-in estimator of the non-iterative conditional expectation g-formula using long short-term memory models». Gli autori sono Jing Li, Sophia M. Rein, Zilong Wang, et al. The non-iterative conditional expectation (NICE) plug-in estimator of the g-formula can be used to estimate the causal effects of sustained treatment strategies, even in the presence of treatment-confounder feedback, under the assumptions of exchangeability, positivity, and consistency. In practical applications, parametric models are commonly used to estimate the joint distribution of confounders, treatments, and outcomes over time.

Il sesto articolo: «More Treatments More Problems: Designing the 2026 American Causal Inference Conference Data Challenge». Gli autori sono George Perrett, Sameer K. Deshpande, Jennifer Hill. This paper documents the design of the 2026 American Causal Inference Conference (ACIC) Data Challenge, which uses a large set of simulations to evaluate the performance of a wide variety of causal inference estimators chosen and implemented by challenge participants. Unlike previous ACIC Data Challenges, which tended to mimic observational studies with a single binary treatment, the 2026 Data Challenge focused on randomized control trials with five arms (one control and four active treatments) to isolate key estimation challenges that arise when simultaneously estimating multiple treatment effects.
